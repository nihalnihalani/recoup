### Why `not_yet_due`, not `needs_facts` (ERR-R1-01)

The finding allowed either outcome. I chose `not_yet_due` for four reasons:

- **The case is not ripe.** With an exemption and a bag confirmed still undelivered, the result depends on an event that has not happened. A delivery gives `not_eligible`. A loss declaration gives `manual_review` under (f)(1)/(f)(2) (L11), or a refund under (f)(3), because 260.5(g) keeps "the right to a refund of bag fees if the bag is lost". The carrier declares a bag lost; elapsed time does not (L7). README cross-pack rule 4 (D147(6)) makes a case that is not ripe `not_yet_due` with a `reevaluate_when`.
- **The contract allows it.** Contract §4 precedence 4b sends only an **unknown** ripeness fact to `needs_facts`. Here the status is known and confirmed. An extracted status still goes to `needs_facts` (R04-15g).
- **`needs_facts` would ask an unanswerable question.** It would ask for a delivery time that the user cannot give while the bag is actually missing.
- **V2-1's concern is not engaged.** V2-1 guards against a positive verdict extrapolated from a status with no as-of time. `not_yet_due` asserts no delay, carries no amount and is not approvable. A status change is a fact change that re-evaluates the case.

**R04-16 stays `needs_facts`.** It has no exemption. A bag truly still undelivered past the threshold would already be significantly delayed, so that case is ripe, and v1 only lacks proof of it.

**The next action is a wait.** The awaited event is not the user's own action (contract §4 4b; D154).

## Item 12: the declared-lost bag and P-260.5-F

I re-read the captured passage. It exempts "the fee for a **significantly delayed bag** in situations where **the delay** resulted from" three passenger actions. Two readings of the captured text are open:

- **The exemptions do not reach a lost bag.** In most of the places where Part 260 covers both kinds of bag, it names them separately: 260.1(b), the 260.5 opening, (b), (c), (e)(1), (e)(2) and 260.10. (f) names only the delayed bag. And 260.5(g) forbids requiring a waiver of "the right to a refund of bag fees if the bag is lost".
- **The exemptions do reach a lost bag.** 260.2 defines a significantly delayed checked bag as one "not delivered ... within 12 hours", and a lost bag literally meets that definition. 260.5(d) also names only the delayed bag, yet this spec applies it to a lost bag (R04-01c; the §10 refund row). That use reads "significantly delayed" in 260.5 as including a lost bag (round-1 correction, ERR-R1-02).

The approved spec (§6, §15 step 2.3) took the second reading without saying so and returned `not_eligible`, which works against the user. No captured passage settles the question. So the correct v1 treatment is `manual_review`: never `not_eligible`, never `eligible`, and an explanation that names both readings (L11).

I also re-fetched the FR 2024-07177 preamble (`federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2024/04/26/2024-07177.txt`). Its SHA-256 `1f3a3196…85bb4` matches the hash already recorded in `sources/federal-register-notices.txt`. Its bag-fee sections "(iii) Exemptions From the Refund Requirement" and "(iv) Waiver of Fee Refunds and Incidental Expenses for Voluntary Separation" do not address (f)(1)/(f)(2) for a lost bag. Because nothing in it settles the point, I captured no new source. Round 1 needed no new source either: every new quotation comes from files already in `sources/`, and each one was checked verbatim against them.
